WHAT IS A CELL?

  • Cellular level — The lowest level of organisation which has life.

  • Structural and functional unit — The cell is the basic building block and functional unit of life.

  • A cell forms the lowest level of organisation which has life.

  • Each kind of cell performs a specific function.
  • Robert Hooke discovered the Cell in 1665.
  • He observed thin slices of cork under a microscope and found box-like compartments.
  • He called these compartments cells.

Discovery of the Cell

  • Microscope — A tool that makes things look 200 to 300 times bigger than what one could see with the unaided eye.

  • Micrographia — The book by Robert Hooke showing detailed drawings of tiny things seen using a microscope.

  • Robert Hooke, an English Scientist, discovered the Cell in 1665.

  • He showed detailed drawings of tiny things in his book "Micrographia".
  • He observed thin slices of cork under a microscope.
  • He found that the cork is made up of box-like compartments, forming a honeycomb structure.
  • This was the first time the word cell was used in science to describe the basic unit of life.

The Cell

  • Unicellular — Organisms made up of only one cell.

  • Multicellular — Organisms made up of many cells.

  • All living organisms are made up of very small compartment-like building blocks called cells.

  • A cell is the smallest unit of life.
  • A cell is capable of performing all biological activities in any living organism.
  • Some organisms like amoeba are unicellular.
  • Most of the organisms are multicellular.

VARIATION IN SHAPE AND STRUCTURE OF CELLS

Size of the Cells

  • Micrometre — A unit of measurement for cell size ($0.1 \times 10^{-6}$ m).
  • Unicellular — A single cell organism.
  • Body size of the organism — The overall size of the living being.

  • Function — The role a cell plays in the body.

  • The size of a cell may vary from 0.1 micrometre to as large as 170 mm x 130 mm.

  • In our body, some blood cells are the smallest and the nerve cells are the longest.
  • The egg cell of ostrich is the largest—about 7 cm in diameter.
  • The size of the cell has no relationship with the body size of the organism.
  • The size of the cell is related to its function.

Shape of the Cells

  • Neurons — Nerve cells that are long, branched and have thread-like projections.

  • Rectangular — Brick-like shape found in plant cells.

  • Different cells have different shapes related to their function and location.

  • Some cells are oval or round, certain others are elongated.
  • Amoeba is irregular in shape, and keeps on changing its shape.
  • The nerve cell is long, branched and has thread-like projections.
  • Plants have rectangular, elongated oval or even tube-like cells.

CELL STRUCTURE AND FUNCTION

Cell Membrane

  • Plasma membrane — Another name for the cell membrane.

  • Cell membrane is also called plasma membrane.

  • It forms the outer boundary of the cell.
  • It is a thin delicate membrane.
  • It separates cells from one another and also from the surrounding medium.
  • It allows only certain selected substances to enter or leave the cell.

Cell Wall

  • Cellulose — Stiff material that makes up the cell wall.

  • Plant cells and bacteria have an extra outer wall surrounding the cell membrane.

  • This is called the cell wall.
  • It is made of stiff material, called cellulose.
  • The cell wall provides protection against temperature variations, atmospheric moisture, and high wind speed.

Nucleus

  • Nuclear membrane — Surrounds the nucleus and controls passage of materials.
  • Nucleoplasm — A dense fluid in the nucleus.
  • Nucleolus — A spherical body present inside the nucleus containing RNA.
  • Chromosomes — Thread-like structures composed of DNA containing genes.

  • Genes — Basic hereditary units responsible for passing genetic characteristics.

  • Nucleus is the dense, oval shaped structure bounded with double layered membrane.

  • It contains genetic materials.
  • The nucleus controls vital functions of the cell.
  • It regulates all activities that occur within the cell and growth.
  • It transmits hereditary characteristics from generation to generation.

Cytoplasm

  • Cell organelles — Small cytoplasmic bodies in cytoplasm that play an active role in functioning.

  • Cytoplasm is a thick jelly-like fluid inside the cell membrane.

  • It occupies the space between the cell membrane and the nucleus.
  • It contains other components of the cell and compounds like carbohydrates, proteins, fats and mineral salts.
  • All the life functions take place in the cytoplasm.
  • There are many small cytoplasmic bodies called cell organelles.

Cell Organelles

  • Mitochondria — Produce energy from food during respiration; power house of the cell.
  • Endoplasmic reticulum — Helps in the transport of substances within the cell.
  • Ribosomes — Help in protein synthesis.
  • Golgi bodies — Synthesize, store and secrete many substances.
  • Vacuoles — Sac-like structures present in the cytoplasm that store soluble food, wastes and secretions.
  • Lysosomes — Present in animal cells and help in cellular digestion.
  • Centrosomes — Present only in animal cells; play a role in reproduction.
  • Plastids — Occur in plant cells only.
  • Chloroplasts — Green plastids due to chlorophyll; help in photosynthesis.
  • Leucoplasts — Colourless plastids; help in storage of food.

  • Chromoplasts — Coloured plastids responsible for colour of flowers and fruits.

DIFFERENCES BETWEEN A PLANT CELL AND AN ANIMAL CELL

  • Cell wall — Present in plant cells, absent in animal cells.
  • Plastids — Present in plant cells, absent in animal cells.

  • Vacuoles — Large in plant cells, small or absent in animal cells.

  • Plant cells have a rigid, non-living cell wall.

  • Plant cells usually have one or two large vacuoles.
  • Plastids are present in plant cells.
  • Animal cells have no cell wall.
  • Vacuoles are either absent or are smaller in size in animal cells.
  • Plastids are absent in animal cells.

WHAT ARE MICROORGANISMS?

  • Microorganisms are very small in size.

  • These cannot be seen with naked eyes.
  • These can be seen with microscope.
  • Microorganisms are found everywhere—in the air, in water, in the soil.
  • Microorganisms are capable of existing in extreme conditions by forming a hard outer covering called cyst.

  • Fungi — Microorganisms found in plural (Fungus singular).

  • Viruses are acellular submicroorganisms that have characteristics of both living and non-living things.
  • Viruses inhabit and reproduce only inside the cells of living organisms.
  • Viruses are smaller than the smallest known cell.
  • Viruses exhibit a variety of shapes such as rod-like, spherical, polygonal etc.

Viruses

  • Viruses obtain their food from the host cells.
  • Viruses are smaller than the smallest known cell.
  • Viruses cannot be seen by simple microscopes.
  • When a virus enters a cell of its host, it multiplies and the host cell bursts.

Increasing Soil Fertility

  • Biological nitrogen fixers — Blue-green algae and symbiotic bacteria.
  • Rhizobium — Symbiotic bacteria living in root nodules of leguminous plants.

  • Nitrogenous compounds — Nitrogen converted into compounds in the soil.

  • Microorganisms like fungi and bacteria in the soil act on plant waste and decompose it into manure.

  • Blue-green algae and symbiotic bacteria are commonly called biological nitrogen fixers.
  • Symbiotic bacteria like Rhizobium absorb atmospheric nitrogen and convert it into nitrogenous compounds.
  • Farmers grow legumes in rotation with other crops to increase nitrogen in the soil.
  • Roots of certain legumes like beans, peas and lentils have root nodules that contain Rhizobium bacteria.

Logic-Based Questions (Page 36)

Question 1: Why are plastic bags not considered eco-friendly? (Hint: decomposition)
Answer:
Plastic bags are not considered eco-friendly because they do not decompose easily. Microorganisms cannot easily break them down into harmless substances like they do with organic waste.

Question 2: Imagine a scenario where microbes stop decomposing for some time. Which of the following would be the effect/effects?
A. Fruits will remain fresh and eatable for many days.
B. There will be thick layers of dried leaves and plant waste.
C. Bodies of dead animals will be lying in the jungle.
Answer:
The effects would be B and C.
Explanation: Microorganisms decompose dead organic waste. If they stop, dead plants and animals would accumulate (B and C). Fruits would not necessarily remain fresh; they might rot differently, but the primary effect is waste accumulation.

Question 3: Why are we able to store dry fruits for a longer period than fresh fruits?
Answer:
Dry fruits have low moisture content. Microorganisms require moisture to grow and reproduce. The lack of water prevents microbial growth, acting as a preservation method similar to adding salt or sugar.

Question 4: Dosa, a dish made of fermented lentils and rice is very famous in the southern part of India which is near the coast. Guess the reason.
Answer:
Dosa is famous because it is made using fermentation. The batter (lentils and rice) is fermented by microorganisms (yeast/bacteria), which makes it soft and fluffy. The coastal climate might also be warm and humid, favoring the fermentation process.

Question 5: "A chicken egg yolk is a single cell which can be seen by the eye." From this statement, is it correct to conclude that the chicken that grows up from this egg is a single cell organism? Why or why not?
Answer:
No, it is not correct.
Explanation: Although the egg yolk is a single cell, the text states: "The egg divides repeatedly to form a complete living being made up of many cells." The chicken is a multicellular organism.

Question 6: Would plants need a cell wall if they could move? Give reason.
Answer:
No, they might not need a rigid cell wall if they could move.
Explanation: The cell wall provides rigidity and protection against temperature variations, moisture, and wind. If plants could move, they would not need to be fixed in place with rigid structures for support against wind. Flexibility would be more important for movement.

Question 7: Uma observed that mango kept outside for two days has rotten, whereas the mango pickle that her mother had made last month is not infected by any microorganism. Why did this happen?
Answer:
The mango kept outside has moisture and nutrients that allow microorganisms to grow and cause rotting. The mango pickle contains salt and sugar, which act as preservatives. High concentration of salt or sugar does not allow microorganisms to grow on them.

Question 8: A soft dough is made of refined flour, warm water, some sugar and yeast powder. Another similar dough is made without sugar and yeast powder. Both are kept in a warm area for about 6-7 hours.

The size of the cells has no relationship with the body size of the organism. The size of the cell is related to its function.

Key Players in Cleansing the Environment

Many microorganisms decompose the dead organic waste of plants and animals into simpler and harmless substances.

Such simpler substances get into the soil and used by other plants. Thus, microorganisms act as the cleansing agent of the nature.

Many bacteria and fungi live in an oxygen-free environment. Some of these bacteria can decompose plant and animal waste present in the environment or household waste water. They release a mixture of gases containing carbon dioxide and high proportion of another gas called methane during this process. This gas has been used as a fuel source for cooking, heating, generate electricity and to even run vehicles.

Anaerobic bacteria (which can live without oxygen) degrade the animal dung into biogas and the left-over slurry can be used as a manure.

WHY IS CELL CONSIDERED TO BE A BASIC UNIT OF LIFE ?

The body of all human beings, plants and animals consist of several million cells. Therefore, they are called multicellular organisms. In multicellular organisms, the various functions in the body are divided among different groups of cells.

Certain organisms consist of only one (single) cell. The single-celled organisms are called unicellular. The microorganisms, bacteria and protozoa are unicellular.

In unicellular organisms, all functions of the body are carried out by a single cell. A single-celled organism amoeba performs all the functions (such as capturing and digesting food, respiration, excretion, growing, reproduction) as done by multicellular organisms using specialised group of cells.

Some microorganisms like algae and fungi contain a few one or more cells. The smaller number of cells in smaller organisms does not in any way affect the functioning of such organisms.

Fungi may be made up of one or more cells. Mould is a multicellular fungus while yeast is a unicellular fungus.

5.2 Checking Divisibility Quickly

  • Shortcuts exist to check divisibility for numbers written in the Indian number system, specifically for 2, 4, 5, 8, and 10.

  • The general form of a number is expressed as a sum of place values, such as ... + 1000d + 100c + 10b + a.
  • All place values except the units place are multiples of 10, meaning divisibility by 10 depends entirely on the units digit.

Divisibility by 10, 5, and 2

  • Units Digit — The last digit of a number in the Indian number system (represented by a in the general form).

  • Shortcut for Divisibility by 9 — A method to identify multiples of 9 by observing digit properties rather than calculating the full quotient.

  • This works because each digit contributes a term that is either 9 * place value or 0 * place value, making every term a multiple of 9.

Think and Discuss 3

Text: If a number is divisible by 7, then all multiples of that number will be divisible by 7.
Answer: Yes, the statement is always true.
Numbers divisible by 7 have 7 as a factor. We can represent such a number as 7j (where j is an integer).
When we take a multiple of this number, we multiply by some integer m. The result is (7j) * m = 7jm.
Since 7jm can be written as 7 * (jm), it still has 7 as a factor. Therefore, it is divisible by 7.
Examples: 14 = 7 * 2, 42 = 7 * 6, 98 = 7 * 14. All multiples of 14 are also divisible by 7.

Math Talk 6

Question: If a number is divisible by both 9 and 4, it must be divisible by 36.
Answer: Yes, the statement is true.
According to the rule discussed in the text, if a number A is divisible by k and also by m, then A is divisible by the LCM of k and m. Here, k = 9 and m = 4. Since 9 and 4 are co-prime numbers (they share no common factors other than 1), their Least Common Multiple (LCM) is their product: 9 * 4 = 36. Therefore, any number divisible by both 9 and 4 must be divisible by 36.

Math Talk 7

Question: If a number is divisible by both 6 and 4, it must be divisible by 24.
Answer: No, the statement is false.
Using the general rule, if a number is divisible by 6 and 4, it must be divisible by the LCM of 6 and 4. The LCM of 6 and 4 is 12, not 24. A number divisible by both 6 and 4 must be a multiple of 12. For example, the number 12 is divisible by 6 and 4, but 12 is not divisible by 24. Thus, the statement is not always true.

Math Talk 8

Question: When you add an odd number to an even number we get a multiple of 6.
Answer: No, the statement is never true.
Multiples of 6 are always even numbers. However, the sum of an odd number and an even number is always an odd number. Therefore, the result can never be a multiple of 6.
Algebraically, let the even number be 2n and the odd number be 2m + 1. Their sum is:
2n + (2m + 1) = 2(n + m) + 1.
This result is of the form 2(integer) + 1, which is odd. Since a multiple of 6 would be of the form 6j (which is even), the equation 2(n + m) + 1 = 6j is impossible.

1. The sum of four consecutive numbers is 34. What are these numbers?

Answer:
Let the four consecutive numbers be n, n + 1, n + 2, and n + 3.
According to the problem, their sum is 34.
So, n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) + (n + 3) = 34.
Combining like terms: 4n + 6 = 34.
Subtract 6 from both sides: 4n = 28.
Divide by 4: n = 7.
The numbers are 7, 8, 9, and 10.
Verification: 7 + 8 + 9 + 10 = 34.

2. Suppose p is the greatest of five consecutive numbers. Describe the other four numbers in terms of p.

Answer:
If p is the greatest of five consecutive numbers, the sequence decreases by 1 for each preceding number.
The five numbers are: p - 4, p - 3, p - 2, p - 1, and p.
Therefore, the other four numbers in terms of p are p - 4, p - 3, p - 2, and p - 1.

3. For each statement below, determine whether it is always true, sometimes true, or never true. Explain your answer. Mention examples and non-examples as appropriate. Justify your claim using algebra.

(i) The sum of two even numbers is a multiple of 3.
Answer: Sometimes true.
Explanation: An even number can be represented as 2k. The sum of two even numbers is 2a + 2b = 2(a + b), which is always even. However, it is not always divisible by 3.

Answer: Sometimes true.
Explanation: Being divisible by 18 implies being divisible by 9 (since 18 = 2 * 9). However, the reverse is not true. A number can be divisible by 9 without being divisible by 18.

5.2 Checking Divisibility Quickly

Earlier, you have learnt shortcuts to check whether a given number, written in the Indian number system is divisible by 2, 4, 5, 8, and 10. Let us revisit them.

Divisibility by 10, 5, and 2: If the units digit of a number is '0', then it is divisible by 10. Let us understand why this works through algebra.

We can write the general form of a number in the Indian system using a set of letter-numbers. For example, a 5-digit number can be expressed as, $edcba$ denoting $e \times 10000 + d \times 1000 + c \times 100 + b \times 10 + a$. The letter-numbers $e, d, c, b,$ and $a$ denote each digit of a 5-digit number.

Any number can be written in general as ...$dcba$, where the letter-numbers $a, b, c$ and $d$ represent the units, tens, hundreds and thousands digit, respectively, and so on. As a sum of place values, this number is —
$$... + 1000d + 100c + 10b + a.$$

(For example, in the number 4075, $d = 4, c = 0, b = 7,$ and $a = 5$.)

We know that each place value, with the exception of the units place, is a multiple of 10. So, $10b, 100c, ...$ all will be multiples of 10. Hence, the number will be divisible by 10 if and only if the units digit $a$ is 0.

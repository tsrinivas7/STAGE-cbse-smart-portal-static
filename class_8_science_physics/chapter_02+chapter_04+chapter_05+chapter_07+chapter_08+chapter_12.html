Chapter 04: Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

Q1. When the switch is turned OFF in the circuit described in Activity 1, what happens to the magnetic needle placed near the wire AB? (1) Chapter 04: Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

✅ Answer: The magnetic needle returns to its original north-south position.

Q2. Name the Danish scientist who discovered the magnetic effect of electric current and state the year of this discovery. (2) Chapter 04: Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

✅ Answer: Hans Christian Oersted, 1819.

Q3. The region around a current-carrying wire where its magnetic effect can be felt is called the __________. (1) Chapter 04: Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

✅ Answer: Magnetic field.

Q4. Assertion (A): A straight copper wire carrying electric current behaves like a magnet. Reason (R): This conclusion is drawn because a magnetic needle placed near the wire gets deflected when current flows through it. (2) Chapter 04: Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

Chapter 05: Force and Number Properties

Q5. Which of the following scenarios best describes a 'pull' force according to the text? (1) Chapter 05: Force and Number Properties

✅ Answer: When the force changes the direction of an object towards you and the object moves towards you.

Q6. What is one effect that a force can have on a body which is currently at rest? (1) Chapter 05: Force and Number Properties

✅ Answer: It can make the stationary body move.

Q7. Force is usually denoted by the letter __________. (1) Chapter 05: Force and Number Properties

✅ Answer: F

Q8. Does applying a force always result in the displacement of an object? Provide an example from the text to justify your answer. (2) Chapter 05: Force and Number Properties

✅ Answer: No, a force does not always make an object move. For example, you can push a large box without displacing it from its position.

Q9. When placing '+' and '−' signs between any four consecutive numbers in all eight possible ways, which property of the resulting expression remains constant regardless of the arrangement? (1) Chapter 05: Force and Number Properties

✅ Answer: The parity (whether the result is even or odd) remains the same.

Q10. According to the text, explain why switching a '+' sign to a '−' sign (for example, changing $+b$ to $-b$) does not alter the parity of the total sum. (2) Chapter 05: Force and Number Properties

✅ Answer: Switching the sign changes the value by twice the number involved (e.g., $2b$). Since twice any integer is an even number, adding or subtracting an even number does not change the parity of the total.

Q11. In the expression $a + b - c - d$, replacing $+b$ with $-b$ results in a change of value equal to __________. (1) Chapter 05: Force and Number Properties

✅ Answer: $2b$

Q12. Anshu explores sums of consecutive numbers and notes that 15 can be expressed as $7 + 8$ and $1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5$. What broader inquiry did Anshu formulate based on these observations regarding natural numbers? (2) Chapter 05: Force and Number Properties

✅ Answer: He asked whether he could write every natural number as a sum of consecutive numbers and which numbers could be written in more than one way.

Q16. Regarding Activity 1 (Beaker A), what observation confirms that sugar is present in the solution even though it is not visible? (1) Chapter 07: Matter in Our Surroundings

Q17. Based on the text, which condition describes the existence of matter in the gaseous state regarding particle motion and force? (1) Chapter 07: Matter in Our Surroundings

Q18. In the experiment where liquid ink is poured gently on the surface of water, what final observation indicates that particles spread into the spaces between water molecules? (2) Chapter 07: Matter in Our Surroundings

Q19. The force of attraction between the particles in any matter is called ______ or interparticle attraction. (1) Chapter 07: Matter in Our Surroundings

Q20. Unlike sugar, when insoluble solids like sand are added to water, the total volume increases. What does this observation imply about the interaction between sand particles and water particles? (1) Chapter 07: Matter in Our Surroundings

Q21. Define 'diffusion' as described in the text. Additionally, provide one example of diffusion in gases mentioned in the chapter. (2) Chapter 07: Matter in Our Surroundings

✅ Answer: Diffusion is the intermixing of particles of two or more substances on their own. An example is smelling the fragrance of burning incense sticks in other rooms.

Q22. The schematic picture labeled (c) in the interparticle spacing diagrams represents the ____ state of matter, where particles are widely spaced apart. (1) Chapter 07: Matter in Our Surroundings

✅ Answer: Gaseous (or Gas)

Q23. According to the text, how does providing heat affect the movement of particles? Give one observation from the potassium permanganate activity that supports this. (2) Chapter 07: Matter in Our Surroundings

✅ Answer: Movement of particles increases when heat is provided. Observation: Potassium permanganate spreads fastest in hot water compared to room temperature or ice-cold water.

Q24. According to the text describing how soap works on oil-stained clothes, what happens to the soap particles relative to the oil particles? (1) Chapter 07: Matter in Our Surroundings

✅ Answer: The soap particles surround the oil particles; one end attaches to the oil and the other mixes with water.

Q25. Based on the table of distinguishing properties, explain why gases are considered highly compressible compared to solids. (2) Chapter 07: Matter in Our Surroundings

✅ Answer: Gases have high compressibility because particles are packed very poorly with most interparticle space, whereas solids have negligible compressibility due to strong interparticle forces and least interparticle space.

Q26. When a solid is heated to change its state into a liquid, the thermal energy increases until it is sufficient to overcome the ____ forces holding the particles together. (1) Chapter 07: Matter in Our Surroundings

✅ Answer: binding

Chapter 08: Geometry of Polygons

Q27. The word 'quadrilateral' comes from Latin words where 'quadri' means __________ and 'latus' refers to __________. (1) Chapter 08: Geometry of Polygons

✅ Answer: Four; Sides

Q28. A student draws a four-sided figure with one curved side. Is this a quadrilateral? Explain your reasoning. (2) Chapter 08: Geometry of Polygons

✅ Answer: No, because a quadrilateral must have exactly four straight sides. A curved side disqualifies it from being classified as a quadrilateral.

Q29. Which of the following statements correctly defines a rectangle according to the chapter? (1) Chapter 08: Geometry of Polygons
(a) All sides are equal and all angles are acute
(b) Opposite sides are equal and all angles are right angles
(c) All angles are different and all sides are different
(d) Only two sides are parallel

✅ Answer: (b) Opposite sides are equal and all angles are right angles

Q30. According to the definition in Section 4.1, a rectangle is a quadrilateral in which all angles are __________ and opposite sides are of __________ length. (1) Chapter 08: Geometry of Polygons

✅ Answer: right angles ($90^{{{{\circ}}}}$); equal

Q31. Observe five figures labeled (i) through (v). Figures (i), (ii), and (iii) are quadrilaterals while (iv) and (v) are not. What specific property makes figures (iv) and (v) NOT quadrilaterals? (2) Chapter 08: Geometry of Polygons

✅ Answer: Figure (iv) has a curved side instead of straight sides, and figure (v) has five sides (making it a pentagon). Both violate the requirement of having exactly four straight sides.

Q32. The text describes Figure (iii) as a specific type of quadrilateral. What is this classification? (1) Chapter 08: Geometry of Polygons

✅ Answer: Concave quadrilateral (also referred to as an arrowhead or dart shape).

✅ Answer: Micrographia.

✅ Answer: The size of the cell has no relationship with the body size of the organism. For example, nerve cells in both rats and elephants are long and branched to perform the same function.

Q35. The bacterium __________ is cited in the text as having the smallest cell of about 0.1 micrometer. (1) Cell Structure and Functions

Q36. Why do muscle cells in the food pipe have a spindle shape? Explain how this shape aids their function. (2) Cell Structure and Functions

✅ Answer: They are spindle-shaped to help them contract and relax in a wave-like manner, pushing the food down to the stomach.

Q39. The cell wall surrounding plant cells is composed of a stiff material called __________, which helps protect the cell against temperature variations and high wind speed. (1) Cell Structure and Functions

Ecosystems and Environment

(A) Parasitism
(B) Commensalism
(C) Mutualism

✅ Answer: Mutualism

Q48. Describe the chain of consequences that occurred in India during the 1980s following the significant export of Indian bullfrog legs. (2) Ecosystems and Environment

✅ Answer: The export led to a decline in the frog population. This caused a rise in agriculture pests because frogs eat insects. Farmers were compelled to use more synthetic pesticides, which harmed the environment, soil, water quality, and human health.

III. SHORT ANSWER TYPE QUESTIONS

Q58. Explain why the number of organisms typically decreases as we move up the trophic levels in a food chain. (2) Ecosystems and Environment

✅ Answer: The number of organisms decreases because energy is lost at each trophic level (approximately 90% is lost as heat/metabolic processes), leaving less energy available to support fewer organisms at higher levels.

Q59. Describe two ways in which human activities can disrupt the natural balance of an ecosystem. (2) Ecosystems and Environment

✅ Answer: Humans disrupt ecosystems through deforestation (removing habitat) and pollution (contaminating air/water/soil), which harms wildlife and alters nutrient cycles.

Q60. Why is the sun considered the ultimate source of energy for almost all ecosystems? (2) Ecosystems and Environment

✅ Answer: Plants (producers) capture solar energy to make food via photosynthesis; this energy is then transferred to consumers when they eat plants or other animals.

IV. CASE-BASED / INTEGRATED QUESTIONS

Read the following paragraph and answer the questions.

Scenario: A farmer decides to use a strong pesticide to kill insects attacking his wheat crop. He sprays the field regularly. After some time, he notices that the birds feeding on the insects in his field are dying. Later, he observes that the soil quality has degraded, and beneficial earthworms are absent.

Q61. Identify the phenomenon where the concentration of the pesticide increases at each step of the food chain, leading to the death of the birds. (1) Ecosystems and Environment

✅ Answer: Biomagnification (or Bioaccumulation).

Q62. Explain how the use of pesticides affected the soil quality and the presence of earthworms. (2) Ecosystems and Environment

✅ Answer: Pesticides are often toxic to non-target organisms like earthworms, killing them. Their absence reduces soil aeration and nutrient cycling, degrading soil quality.

Q63. Suggest one eco-friendly alternative method the farmer could use to control insect pests without harming the ecosystem. (2) Ecosystems and Environment

✅ Answer: Biological control (using natural predators like ladybugs) or Integrated Pest Management (IPM) involving crop rotation and resistant varieties.

VI. LONG ANSWER / CONCEPT BASED QUESTIONS

Q70. Construct a food chain consisting of four organisms starting with a producer. Then, predict what would happen to the other organisms if the second organism in your chain were removed. (3) Ecosystems and Environment

✅ Answer: Example: Grass → Rabbit → Snake → Eagle. If the rabbit is removed, the snake population will decrease due to lack of food, and the eagle population may also decline or switch prey. The grass population will likely increase due to reduced grazing pressure.

Q71. Discuss the importance of biodiversity for the stability of an ecosystem. What might happen if a single species becomes extinct? (3) Ecosystems and Environment

✅ Answer: Biodiversity ensures resilience; if one species fails, others can fill its role. Extinction can break food chains, reduce genetic diversity, and destabilize nutrient cycles, potentially leading to ecosystem collapse.

Q72. Compare and contrast a natural ecosystem with an artificial ecosystem. Give one example of each and mention one key difference in their maintenance requirements. (3) Ecosystems and Environment

✅ Answer: Natural ecosystems (e.g., Forest) sustain themselves through natural processes. Artificial ecosystems (e.g., Crop Field/Terrarium) require human intervention for maintenance like watering, fertilizing, or pest control.

Q73. Explain the role of decomposers in maintaining the cleanliness of our environment. What would happen if decomposers were completely absent? (3) Ecosystems and Environment

✅ Answer: Decomposers recycle nutrients and remove dead waste. Without them, dead bodies and waste would pile up, nutrients would remain locked in dead matter, and soil fertility would drop drastically.

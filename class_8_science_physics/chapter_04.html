Electricity : Magnetic and Heating Effects

effects of electricity — Different effects produced when electricity flows through a conductor, such as heat, magnetism, or chemical changes.
heat — One of the effects produced when electric current passes through a conductor.
magnetism — One of the effects produced when electric current passes through a conductor.
chemical changes — One of the effects produced when electric current passes through a conductor.

  • Electricity is a useful form of energy used in homes, offices, and factories.
  • When electric current flows through a conductor, it produces heat, magnetism, or chemical changes.
  • Understanding these effects helps us know how electrical appliances like bulbs, heaters, and fans work.
  • This chapter focuses on the heating and magnetic effects of electric current.

DOES AN ELECTRIC CURRENT HAVE A MAGNETIC EFFECT ?

magnetic field — The region around a magnet or a current-carrying wire where its magnetic effect can be felt.

  • Hans Christian Oersted discovered in 1819 that an electric current produces a magnetic field around a conductor.
  • When current flows through a wire, it acts like a magnet and can deflect a magnetic needle.
  • The magnetic effect disappears once the current stops flowing.
  • This effect can act through non-magnetic materials placed between the wire and the magnet.

Showing the magnetic effect of current

  • A thin copper wire connected to a battery and a key is placed in the north-south direction.
  • A magnetic needle placed near the wire deflects when the switch is turned ON.
  • The needle returns to its original north-south position when the switch is turned OFF.
  • Reversing the direction of current also reverses the direction of deflection.

Electromagnets

Electromagnets — Temporary magnets that show magnetic properties only as long as electric current flows through their coil.
magnetic properties — The ability of a material to attract magnetic substances like iron, which electromagnets possess only when current is flowing.

  • Electromagnets are made by winding insulated copper wire around a soft iron core.
  • They are temporary magnets because they lose magnetism when the current is switched off.
  • The strength of an electromagnet increases with the number of turns in the coil.
  • The strength also increases with the strength of the current flowing through the coil.
  • The nature of the core material affects strength; soft iron produces very strong electromagnets.

Uses of Electromagnets

  • Lifting heavy loads: Used in cranes in factories and scrap-yards to lift and sort iron/steel objects.
  • In hospitals: Used to remove iron splinters from eyes during eye injury treatments.
  • Separation: Used to separate magnetic substances (like iron, nickel, cobalt) from non-magnetic substances (like copper, plastic).
  • Instruments: Used in electric bells, telegraphic systems, speakers, and audio players.
  • The poles of an electromagnet can be reversed by changing the direction of the current.

DOES A CURRENT CARRYING WIRE GET HOT ?

heating effect of current — The generation or production of heat in a resistor or conductor when electricity passes through it.

  • When electric current flows through a resistor (like a heating coil), it gets heated up.
  • The heat produced depends on the current passed, the time for which current flows, and the nature of the material.
  • Copper wires used for connections do not get hot because copper is a good conductor with low resistance.
  • Tube lights and CFLs do not generate heat and are called cold sources of light.

Applications of the Heating Effect of Current

  • Electrical Heating Appliances: Devices like electric irons, water heaters, geysers, and toasters use heating coils made of high resistance wire like nichrome.
  • Electric Filament Bulb: Inside the bulb, a thin tungsten filament gets heated up to white hot and emits light.
  • Electric Fuse: A safety device made from a low-melting alloy that melts to break the circuit during excessive current.
  • Industrial Use: Used in steel manufacturing industries to produce high heat to melt and recycle scrap steel.

Electrical Heating Appliances

  • These appliances are fitted with heating coils made of high resistance wire such as nichrome wire.
  • Nichrome is an alloy containing iron, nickel, chromium, and manganese that does not melt at high temperatures.
  • Visible heating elements in some appliances glow red hot when current flows through them.
  • Examples include electric irons, room heaters, toasters, and hot plates.

Electric Filament Bulb

  • The filament is made from a very thin high resistance tungsten wire.
  • When current flows, the filament gets heated up, becomes white hot, and starts emitting light.
  • The filament is enclosed in a glass shell to protect it and maintain the necessary conditions for glowing.

Electric Fuse

  • An electric fuse is a safety device that prevents damage to appliances and possible fires.
  • Fuse wire is made from a low-melting alloy that melts away when large current passes through the circuit.
  • When the fuse wire melts, the circuit is broken, preventing further damage to electrical appliances.
  • Appropriate wires, plugs, and sockets rated for the specified electric current should be used.

HOW DOES A BATTERY GENERATE ELECTRICITY ?

Voltaic cell — Also known as a Galvanic cell, it is the first battery invented using metals and liquid to generate electric current.
Galvanic Cell — Another name for the voltaic cell, named after scientists Alessandro Volta and Luigi Galvani.
electrolyte — A liquid, usually a weak acid or salt solution, placed in a container with metal plates to produce electricity.
electrodes — The two metal plates made of different materials that are partly dipped in the electrolyte.

  • A voltaic cell contains two metal plates (electrodes) and an electrolyte in a glass or plastic container.
  • A chemical reaction between the plates and the electrolyte produces electricity.
  • Electric current flows from the positive terminal to the negative terminal through the circuit.
  • When the chemicals get used up, the cell stops working and is called "dead".
  • You can make a simple voltaic cell at home using lemons, copper wires, and iron nails.

Dry Cell (or Battery cell)

Dry Cell — A source of electricity that generates direct current (DC) due to a chemical reaction inside it; also called a battery cell.
zinc container — The outer container of a dry cell which acts as the negative terminal.
negative terminal — The terminal in a dry cell made from the zinc container where current flows out.
carbon rod — A rod placed at the centre of the zinc container which acts as the positive terminal.
positive terminal — The terminal in a dry cell made from the carbon rod with a metallic cap.

  • A single dry cell gives a voltage of 1.5 volt and is available in different sizes and shapes.
  • The carbon rod is surrounded by a mixture of manganese dioxide and powdered charcoal.
  • The remaining space is filled with a thick paste of ammonium chloride.
  • Dry cells are light, small, and have no fear of leakage, but they are single-use and must be disposed of.
  • Rechargeable batteries are increasingly used to overcome the single-use disadvantage of dry cells.

Rechargeable Batteries

Rechargeable Batteries — Batteries that can be recharged and reused multiple times, preventing wastage and saving money.

  • They are used to overcome the disadvantage of single-use dry cells.
  • They can be recharged and reused multiple times.
  • Using rechargeable batteries prevents wastage and saves money.
  • Examples include batteries used in laptops and mobile phones.

Electricity is commonly used everywhere—at home, offices, factories etc. It is an extremely useful form of energy. But electricity does not just provide light and power—it also produces different effects when it flows through a conductor. These are known as the effects of electricity.

When electric current passes through a conductor, it can produce heat, magnetism, or chemical changes. These effects are used in many useful devices like electric bulbs, heaters, fans, electromagnets, and even in processes like electroplating. Understanding the effects of electricity helps us know how various electrical appliances work and how electricity makes our lives easier and more efficient.

In this chapter, we will discuss the heating and magnetic effects of electric current.

DOES AN ELECTRIC CURRENT HAVE A MAGNETIC EFFECT ?

A Danish scientist Hans Christian Oersted in 1819 discovered that when an electric current (DC) is passed through any conductor, a magnetic field is produced around it.

Magnetic field is defined as the region around a magnet or a current carrying wire where its magnetic effect can be felt.

Showing the magnetic effect of current

  • Connect a thin copper wire to a battery through a key, so that the section AB of the wire is in the north-south direction.
  • Place a magnetic needle near the wire AB and let it settle in the north-south direction.
  • Turn the switch ON to pass current through the wire. What happens to the magnetic needle ?
  • The needle gets deflected from its original position.
  • Now turn the switch OFF and see what happens ?
  • Magnetic needle returns to its original north-south position.
  • Repeat the experiment by reversing the direction of current, and see what happens.

CONCLUSION : From this experiment, it is concluded that when electric current is passed through a wire, it acts like a magnet.

As we have learnt (in the chapter 'Exploring Magnets', Class-6). The compass needle is a tiny magnet which deflects when a magnet is brought near it. This magnetic effect can act through any non-magnetic materials kept it between. The deflection indicates that the current carrying wire has a magnetic effect on the compass needle. This magnetic effect disappears once the current stops and the compass needle returns to its original direction. The region where the compass needle was deflected was the magnetic field because the magnetic effect was felt there.

Magnetic effect of electric current is a phenomenon that occurs when electric current flows through a conductor (e.g., a wire) which produces a magnetic field around it. As soon as the current stops flowing, the magnetic field disappears.

Electromagnets, electric bells, motors, fans, loud speakers are some practical applications of the magnetic effect of electric current.

Electromagnets

Electromagnets are based on the magnetic effect of current. An electromagnet shows magnetic properties only as long as the electric current flows through its coil. Thus, electromagnets are temporary magnets. Electromagnets of very high strength can be made easily.

An electromagnet is usually prepared by winding a large number of turns of an insulated copper wire on a soft iron core.

Heating Effect of Electric Current

When electric current flows through a resistor, such as, a heating coil of nichrome wire, it gets heated up.

The generation (production) of heat in a resistor (or conductor) when electricity passes through it is called heating effect of current.

The heat produced in a resistor on passing current, is depends on the following factors :

  • Current passed through the resistor
  • Time (or duration) for which the current is passed
  • Nature of the material.

HOW DOES A BATTERY GENERATE ELECTRICITY ?

Voltaic cell or Galvanic Cell

Invention

The voltaic cell is also known as Galvanic cell. It gets its name from two Italian scientists, Alessandro Volta and Luigi Galvani. In the late 1700s, Galvani observed that when a dead frog's leg was touched with copper or iron metals, it kicked. Galvani thought that the electricity came from the frog itself because electricity could stimulate muscular motion. But Volta believed that the electricity came from metals. To check his theory, he used saltwater-soaked paper instead of the frog's leg and still got the electric current.

This proved that it was a combination of metals and liquid which generated electric current. This led to the invention of the first battery.

Composition and Working

A voltaic cell contains two metal plates made of different materials and a liquid called an electrolyte. This setup is placed in a glass or a plastic container. The plates are called electrodes. They are partly dipped in an electrolyte, which is usually a weak acid or salt solution. A chemical reaction takes place between the plates and the electrolyte, which produces electricity. When the circuit is connected, electric current flows from positive terminal to the negative terminal through the circuit.

When the chemicals get used up, the cell stops working. It is then called "dead" and cannot supply any more electricity.

You can make your own voltaic cell using some simple easily available materials.

Advantages of Dry Cells
Dry cells have the following advantages :

Disadvantage of Dry Cell
The biggest disadvantage of a dry cell is that it is a single use cell. Once it is used up, it has to be disposed off.

Rechargeable Batteries
In order to overcome the disadvantage of single-use dry cell, rechargeable batteries are used. They can be recharge and reuse multiple times which prevents wastage and saves money.

Chapter 13

Chapter Summary

WHY IS EARTH A UNIQUE PLANET ?

Term — Earth’s crust: The outermost shell of the Earth, thin (40 km deep), containing all known life, making up 1% of Earth’s mass.
Term — Gravity: The force that pulls everything towards the Earth, allowing us to stand, pump blood, and sustain life.
Term — Greenhouse effect: The process where gases like carbon dioxide trap heat in the atmosphere, maintaining Earth’s temperature for life.

  • Earth is the only planet with life, due to its unique combination of atmosphere, gravity, and distance from the Sun.
  • The Earth’s crust is thin and fragile, like the skin of an apple, and holds all known life.
  • Gravity keeps the atmosphere from escaping into space, which is essential for life.
  • The greenhouse effect maintains a stable temperature, preventing Earth from becoming too cold or too hot.
  • Earth’s position in the solar system allows water to remain in liquid form, essential for life.

WHAT DO THE PLANETS OF OUR SOLAR SYSTEM LOOK LIKE ?

Term — Terrestrial planets: The four innermost planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) that are small and rocky.
Term — Jovian planets: The four outer planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune) that are large and gaseous.
Term — Greenhouse effect: The process where gases like carbon dioxide trap heat in the atmosphere, maintaining Earth’s temperature for life.

  • The inner planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) are rocky and small, while the outer planets are large and gaseous.
  • Venus is the hottest planet (450°C) due to its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere, which traps heat (greenhouse effect).
  • Earth’s average temperature is 15°C, suitable for life, while planets farther from the Sun get colder.
  • The greenhouse effect is crucial for maintaining Earth’s temperature, but Venus’s extreme greenhouse effect makes it uninhabitable.

WHAT MAKES THE EARTH SUITABLE FOR LIFE TO EXIST ?

Term — Habitable zone (Goldilocks zone): The range of distances from a star where water remains liquid.
Term — Ozone layer: A layer in the atmosphere that blocks harmful ultraviolet (UV) rays from the Sun.
Term — Geosphere: The solid part of the Earth, including rocks, soils, and minerals.
Term — Biosphere: The region of the Earth that encompasses all living organisms and their habitats.

  • Earth’s position in the habitable zone allows water to remain in liquid form, essential for life.
  • Earth’s size and gravity are just right to retain an atmosphere and prevent it from escaping.
  • The ozone layer protects life from harmful UV rays, which can cause skin cancer and damage ecosystems.
  • The geosphere provides nutrients and materials for life, while the biosphere includes all living organisms.

WHAT ALLOWS LIFE TO BE SUSTAINED ON EARTH ?

Term — Hydrosphere: All the water on Earth, including oceans, rivers, lakes, and groundwater.
Term — Geosphere: The solid part of the Earth, including rocks, soils, and minerals.
Term — Biosphere: The region of the Earth that encompasses all living organisms and their habitats.

  • Air, water, sunlight, and soil interact to support life on Earth.
  • Plants use sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water to make food through photosynthesis, releasing oxygen.
  • The greenhouse effect traps heat, keeping water in liquid form and maintaining Earth’s temperature.
  • The hydrosphere supports aquatic life, while the geosphere provides nutrients and minerals.
  • The biosphere includes all living organisms, and their interactions maintain ecological balance.

WHAT KEEPS LIFE FROM DISAPPEARING ?

Term — Reproduction: The process by which living organisms produce new individuals of the same species.
Term — Genes: Instructions passed from parents to offspring that determine physical traits and development.
Term — Asexual reproduction: Reproduction without seeds, where one parent produces offspring.
Term — Sexual reproduction: Reproduction involving the fusion of male and female gametes to form seeds.

  • Reproduction ensures the continuity of life and allows species to adapt to changing environments.
  • Genes passed from parents to offspring determine physical traits and ensure offspring resemble parents.
  • Asexual reproduction produces clones, while sexual reproduction introduces genetic variation.
  • Reproduction allows species to adapt to new environments and survive over time.

MODES OF REPRODUCTION IN PLANTS

Term — Asexual reproduction: Reproduction without seeds, where one parent produces offspring.
Term — Sexual reproduction: Reproduction involving the fusion of male and female gametes to form seeds.
Term — Gametes: Specialized reproductive cells produced by male and female parents.
Term — Vegetative propagation: Asexual reproduction where plants reproduce using vegetative parts like stems, roots, or leaves.

  • Plants reproduce through asexual and sexual methods.
  • Asexual reproduction includes methods like cell fission, budding, spore formation, fragmentation, and vegetative propagation.
  • Sexual reproduction involves the fusion of male and female gametes to form seeds.
  • Vegetative propagation allows plants to reproduce without seeds and produce clones.

ASEXUAL REPRODUCTION IN PLANTS

Term — Cell fission: Splitting of a cell into two or more cells of the same type.
Term — Budding: Reproduction where a bud forms on the parent organism and detaches to become an independent individual.
Term — Spore formation: Reproduction where spores are produced and germinate to form new plants.
Term — Fragmentation: Reproduction where a body breaks into fragments, each growing into a new individual.

  • Cell fission is common in unicellular organisms like bacteria and amoeba.
  • Budding occurs in yeast, where a bud forms and detaches to become a new individual.
  • Spore formation is common in fungi and bacteria, where spores are protected and germinate under favorable conditions.
  • Fragmentation occurs in algae like spirogyra, where the body breaks into fragments that grow into new individuals.

SEXUAL REPRODUCTION IN FLOWERING PLANTS

Term — Stamen: The male reproductive part of a flower, consisting of filament and anther.
Term — Pistil: The female reproductive part of a flower, consisting of stigma, style, and ovary.
Term — Pollination: The transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma.
Term — Fertilisation: The fusion of male and female gametes to form a zygote.

  • The stamen produces pollen grains, while the pistil contains ovules with eggs.
  • Pollination is the transfer of pollen from the anther to the stigma, which can occur by wind, water, or insects.
  • Fertilisation occurs when the male and female gametes fuse to form a zygote.
  • The zygote develops into a seed, and the ovary matures into a fruit.

DISPERSEL OF SEEDS AND FRUITS

Term — Wind dispersal: Seeds dispersed by wind, often light or with tufts of hair.
Term — Water dispersal: Seeds dispersed by water, often buoyant or with structures for floating.
Term — Animal dispersal: Seeds dispersed by animals, often with hooks, spines, or edible pulp.

  • Wind dispersal includes seeds like dandelion, maple, and sunflower.
  • Water dispersal includes seeds like coconut, lily, and lotus.
  • Animal dispersal includes seeds with hooks or spines, like tribulus and xanthium, or edible fruits like coconut.

MODES OF REPRODUCTION IN ANIMALS

Term — Sexual reproduction: Reproduction involving two parents, male and female.
Term — Asexual reproduction: Reproduction by a single organism without a mate.
Term — Gametes: Specialized reproductive cells produced by male and female parents.
Term — Fertilisation: The fusion of male and female gametes to form a zygote.

  • Sexual reproduction involves two parents, while asexual reproduction involves one parent.
  • Examples of sexual reproduction include humans, cows, and frogs.
  • Examples of asexual reproduction include amoeba, yeast, and hydra.
  • Fertilisation can be external (in water) or internal (in the female body).

SEXUAL REPRODUCTION IN ANIMALS

Term — Fertilisation: The fusion of male and female gametes to form a zygote.
Term — External fertilisation: Fertilisation that occurs outside the female body.
Term — Internal fertilisation: Fertilisation that occurs inside the female body.
Term — Viviparous: Animals that give birth to young ones.
Term — Oviparous: Animals that lay eggs.

  • Fertilisation can be external (in fish and frogs) or internal (in mammals and birds).
  • In humans, fertilisation occurs in the female body, and the embryo develops in the uterus.
  • Viviparous animals give birth to young ones, while oviparous animals lay eggs.
  • The embryo develops into a foetus, which is born when fully developed.

ASEXUAL REPRODUCTION IN ANIMALS

Term — Binary fission: Reproduction where one cell splits into two daughter cells.
Term — Multiple fission: Reproduction where a cell produces many daughter cells.
Term — Budding: Reproduction where a bud forms on the parent organism and detaches to become an independent individual.

  • Binary fission occurs in amoeba, where the nucleus divides and the cell splits into two.
  • Multiple fission occurs in malarial parasites, where the cell divides many times within a cyst.
  • Budding occurs in hydra and yeast, where a bud forms and detaches to become a new individual.

WHAT ARE THE THREATS TO LIFE ON EARTH ?

Term — Climate change: Long-term shifts in temperature and weather patterns.
Term — Biodiversity loss: Decline or disappearance of biological diversity.
Term — Pollution: Contamination of air, water, and soil by harmful substances.

  • Climate change is caused by burning fossil fuels, leading to global warming and extreme weather.
  • Biodiversity loss is caused by habitat destruction, pollution, and climate change.
  • Pollution affects air, water, and soil, leading to health problems and ecosystem disruption.

STRATEGIES FOR A SUSTAINABLE FUTURE

Term — Renewable energy: Energy from sources like solar and wind that can be replenished.
Term — Organic fertilizers: Fertilizers made from natural sources, reducing pollution.
Term — Recycling: Reusing materials to reduce waste and pollution.
Term — Global agreements: International treaties to protect the environment, like the Montreal Protocol and Paris Agreement.

  • Using renewable energy, planting trees, and reducing pollution can help preserve the environment.
  • Global agreements aim to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and protect biodiversity.
  • Individuals can contribute by recycling, reducing waste, and using environmentally friendly transportation.

KEY WORDS

Term — Greenhouse effect: Process through which heat is trapped near Earth's surface by greenhouse gases.
Term — Habitable zone (Goldilocks zone): The range of distances from the Sun (or another star) over which water remains liquid.
Term — Ozone layer: Layer in the atmosphere that acts as a shield, blocking harmful ultraviolet (UV) rays from the Sun that can damage life on Earth.
Term — Cosmic rays: Tiny, high energy particles coming from space and hitting the Earth.
Term — Solar wind: Tiny, high energy charged particles coming to the Earth from the Sun.
Term — Hydrosphere: All the water that covers the Earth's surface in the form of different water bodies.
Term — Geosphere: The solid parts of the Earth, including materials like rocks, soils and minerals.
Term — Geodiversity: The natural variety of geological (i.e., rocks, minerals, and fossils) features, soil, hydrological features (water bodies) along with landforms and processes that shape them.
Term — Biosphere: The sum of all ecosystems on Earth, representing the thin zone where life exists.
Term — Genetic material (genes): Instructions by parents to their offspring about the process to develop from a single cell, which are stored inside every cell of a living being.
Term — Vegetative propagation: A kind of asexual reproduction where plants reproduce when any of their parts (leaf, stem or root) is planted in the soil.
Term — Gametes: Specialised reproductive cells made by each parent.
Term — Pollination: The process of pollens being carried to another flower by wind, insects or animals.
Term — Fertilisation: Combination of male and female gamete to form a zygote that becomes a seed.

Chapter 13: Our Home — Earth, a Unique Life-Sustaining Planet

Let's Revise! — Understanding

1. What is the uppermost layer of the Earth called?

The uppermost layer of the Earth is called the crust.

2. What is the position of planet Earth from the Sun?

Earth is the third planet from the Sun.

3. Name the hottest planet of the solar system.

The hottest planet in the solar system is Venus.

What Makes the Earth Suitable for Life to Exist?

1. What makes the Earth a habitable zone?

Earth is located in the habitable zone (also called the Goldilocks zone) — the region around the Sun where temperatures are just right for water to remain in liquid form, which is essential for life.

2. What would have happened to human beings if the Earth’s gravity was extremely strong?

If Earth’s gravity were extremely strong, it would pull us down with such force that our bones would crush.

3. What protects us against cosmic rays?

Earth’s magnetic field protects us by deflecting harmful cosmic rays and solar wind particles, preventing them from reaching the surface directly.

What Allows Life to Be Sustained on Earth?

1. How is air important for us?

Air is important because it contains oxygen, which is essential for respiration in humans, animals, and plants. It also helps regulate temperature through the greenhouse effect.

2. What does hydrosphere encompass?

The hydrosphere encompasses all the water on Earth — in the form of oceans, rivers, lakes, ponds, groundwater, and even water vapour in the atmosphere.

3. What do you mean by geosphere?

The geosphere is the solid part of the Earth, including rocks, soils, minerals, and landforms. It provides nutrients for plants and materials for construction.

4. What is biosphere made up of?

The biosphere is made up of all living organisms — plants, animals, microorganisms — and their habitats. It is the zone where life exists on Earth.

What Keeps Life from Disappearing?

1. Name two organisms which reproduce by asexual method.

Two organisms that reproduce by asexual method are Amoeba and Yeast.

2. What is the splitting of a cell into two or more cells of the same type called?

The splitting of a cell into two or more cells of the same type is called cell fission.

3. Name an organism that reproduces by fragmentation.

An organism that reproduces by fragmentation is Spirogyra.

4. How does a spirogyra reproduce?

Spirogyra reproduces by fragmentation — its long ribbon-like body breaks into fragments, and each fragment grows into a new individual.

Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

1. Name the male and the female reproductive part of a flower.

The male reproductive part of a flower is the stamen, and the female reproductive part is the pistil.

2. Name the process during which male and female gametes unite to form a zygote.

The process during which male and female gametes unite to form a zygote is called fertilisation.

3. Name a seed dispersed by the wind.

A seed dispersed by the wind is the dandelion seed, which has a parachute-like structure to help it float.

Modes of Reproduction in Animals

1. Name the gametes produced by the male and the female human beings.

The male produces sperm, and the female produces ovum (egg).

2. Name the process of fusion of egg and the sperm.

The process of fusion of egg and sperm is called fertilisation.

3. What are the animals which give birth to young ones called?

Animals that give birth to young ones are called viviparous animals.

Asexual Reproduction in Animals

1. Name two organisms which reproduce by asexual method.

Two organisms that reproduce by asexual method are Amoeba and Yeast.

2. Name two methods of asexual reproduction.

Two methods of asexual reproduction are binary fission and budding.

3. Name the method by which Amoeba reproduces?

Amoeba reproduces by binary fission.

4. By what name are the animals which lay eggs called?

Animals that lay eggs are called oviparous animals.

What Are the Threats to Life on Earth?

1. What is the triple planetary crisis?

The triple planetary crisis refers to three major global environmental problems: climate change, biodiversity loss, and pollution.

2. Name some fossil fuels.

Some fossil fuels are coal, oil, and gas.

3. Why should harmful chemicals be properly treated before being released into the air?

Harmful chemicals should be properly treated before being released into the air because they can cause air pollution, lead to health problems (like asthma and bronchitis), and contribute to global warming and acid rain.

  16. Answer: Without Earth’s magnetic field, cosmic rays and solar wind would directly hit the atmosphere, potentially depleting the ozone layer and allowing harmful UV rays to reach the surface. This could damage living cells, increasing risks like skin cancer and harming plants and animals.

  18. Answer:

    • Liquid Water — essential for drinking, growing crops, and supporting life.
    • Habitable Atmosphere — oxygen-rich for respiration, protected from Mars’ thin, carbon dioxide-heavy atmosphere.
    • Temperature Control — to maintain Earth-like temperatures (Mars’ average is -60°C).
    • Hardest to replicate: Creating a habitable atmosphere — Mars’ atmosphere is 100 times thinner than Earth’s.

    • Answer:
    • Cause: Climate change, driven by increased greenhouse gases from burning fossil fuels.
    • Adaptation Strategies:
      • Water conservation: Implement rainwater harvesting and efficient irrigation.
      • Sustainable farming: Use drought-resistant crops and sustainable practices.

  20. If there were no atmosphere on the Earth, would it affect life, temperature, and water on the planet? Explain.

    • Answer:
    • Life: Impossible without oxygen; UV rays would damage cells.
    • Temperature: Earth would become extremely cold, similar to the Moon.
    • Water: Liquid water would evaporate or freeze due to extreme temperature swings and low pressure.

  21. Discuss five examples of vegetative propagation.

    • Answer:
    • Money Plant: Stem cutting with a node grows roots and becomes a new plant.

    • Sugarcane: Stem cuttings with nodes are planted to produce new sugarcane plants.
    • Bryophyllum: Adventitious buds on leaves develop into new plants.

Key Words

  • Greenhouse effect: Process through which heat is trapped near Earth's surface by greenhouse gases.
  • Habitable zone (Goldilocks zone): The range of distances from the Sun (or another star) over which water remains liquid.
  • Ozone layer: Layer in the atmosphere that acts as a shield, blocking harmful ultraviolet (UV) rays from the Sun that can damage life on Earth.
  • Cosmic rays: Tiny, high energy particles coming from space and hitting the Earth.
  • Solar wind: Tiny, high energy charged particles coming to the Earth from the Sun.
  • Hydrosphere: All the water that covers the Earth's surface in the form of different water bodies.
  • Geosphere: The solid parts of the Earth, including materials like rocks, soils and minerals.
  • Geodiversity: The natural variety of geological (i.e., rocks, minerals, and fossils) features, soil, hydrological features (water bodies) along with landforms and processes that shape them.
  • Biosphere: The sum of all ecosystems on Earth, representing the thin zone where life exists.
  • Genetic material (genes): Instructions by parents to their offspring about the process to develop from a single cell, which are stored inside every cell of a living being.
  • Vegetative propagation: A kind of asexual reproduction where plants reproduce when any of their parts (leaf, stem or root) is planted in the soil.
  • Gametes: Specialised reproductive cells made by each parent.
  • Pollination: The process of pollens being carried to another flower by wind, insects or animals.
  • Fertilisation: Combination of male and female gamete to form a zygote that becomes a seed.

WHAT DO THE PLANETS OF OUR SOLAR SYSTEM LOOK LIKE ?

Out of the eight planets that move around the Sun in their own orbits, the four inner most planets—Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars are relatively small and are called terrestrial planets. They have well defined surface containing rocks and soil.

The other four planets—Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune are relatively larger and are called jovian planets. They are mostly gaseous.

Mercury, the planet nearest to the Sun has an average temperature of 170°C.

The second nearest planet to the Sun is Venus. Naturally, it would be expected for Mercury to be the hottest planet considering its proximity to the Sun. But you will be surprised to know that Venus has the highest average temperature of 450°C and is the hottest planet.

Venus has a thick atmosphere consisting of mainly carbon dioxide and small quantity of oxygen and water vapour. Its thick atmosphere traps the air and does not let the heat escape. This is called the greenhouse effect which makes Venus the hottest planet.

Gases like carbon dioxide in the Earth's atmosphere trap heat by absorbing the radiation given off by the Earth, after it gets warmed by the Sun. So, we can say that greenhouse effect plays an important role in maintaining an ideal temperature on the Earth for life to survive.

But this greenhouse effect is not the same principle on which a greenhouse for plants at cool climate works. A plant greenhouse traps warm air due to its closed space, usually glass walls.

So, in both the cases, the atmosphere is kept warm, but it is done differently.

Average temperature of the Earth is 15°C. As we move away from the Sun, planets get colder.

WHAT KEEPS LIFE FROM DISAPPEARING ?

Reproduction by living organisms ensures the continuity of life on Earth. The production of new individual from the parents of the same species is called reproduction. All living organisms reproduce to maintain the existence of their species. Usually, the young ones that animals produce look like them. That is because genes play an important role in determining physical traits. Genes are the instructions passed by parents to their offspring about how to develop from a single cell. Genes are stored in every cell of a living being. These instructions guide the formation of the complete body structure, internal organs and functions of each organ. This ensures that the young one (calf, kitten or pup) grows up to be like its parent (cow, cat or dog).

Reproduction also allows for some changes in the instructions that are passed down from parents to offspring so that they survive better in a new environment. For example, camels adapted to survive in desert by developing humps to store fat. With the development of antibiotic, some bacteria have become resistant to them.

Such type of changes, over generations, lead to the development of new features or even completely new types of living beings.

So, reproduction :
- continues the existence of species
- helps the species to adapt to changes
- continues in new forms.

Cell Fission

Splitting of a mature cell into two, or more cells of the same type is called cell fission. It is the most common method of asexual reproduction in unicellular organisms such as bacteria and amoeba.

Budding

Yeast (a non-green plant) reproduces by budding. A bulb-like projection formed on the body of an organism is known as the bud. The nucleus of the parent body divides into two, and one of the nuclei goes into the bud. When the bud gets matured, it detaches itself from the parent body and becomes an independent individual.

Spore Formation

Some bacteria and lower forms of life reproduce through spores. This is the most common method of asexual reproduction in most of the fungi and bacteria.

The spore is a small single-celled reproductive body which is covered by a thick and hard protective coating. This protective layer helps it to survive under unfavourable conditions, such as extremely high temperature, lack of food and water etc. When the conditions become favourable, the thick coating breaks open and the spores germinate to produce new plants.

The mucor, fern, moss and bread mould (Rhizopus) reproduce asexually by spore formation.

SEXUAL REPRODUCTION IN ANIMALS

Most animals (both single-celled and multi-cellular) reproduce by sexual reproduction method. In sexual reproduction two parents—one male and one female, are involved. Each parent produces one reproductive cell called gamete. The male produces the sperm and the female produces the egg or ovum. Such organisms are called unisexual.

Cockroaches, frogs, fish, birds, reptiles and humans are unisexual.

  • In higher organisms, there are special organs to produce these gametes and to allow fusion of these cells.
  • Some organisms (earthworm, leeches, hydra) have both the male and the female sex organs present in the same body. Such organisms are called hermaphrodites or bisexual.
  • In Monocystic, the two individuals taking part in sexual reproduction are similar in shape and size.
  • In malarial parasite, the two individuals are dissimilar.

Fertilisation

When the sperm (from male partner) and ovum (from female partner) fuse together, a new cell, called zygote, is formed.

The process of fusion of the sperm and the ovum (or egg) is called fertilisation of the egg.

Fertilisation takes place in two different ways. These are external fertilisation and internal fertilisation.

External Fertilisation

When the fusion of male gametes with female gametes takes place outside the body of the female partner, the fertilisation is called external fertilisation.

In frogs and fishes, the male and the female partners release their gametes in water, and the fertilisation takes place in water.

Internal Fertilisation

When the fusion of the gametes of the male and the female takes place inside the body of the female partner, the fertilisation is called internal fertilisation.

In Cats, Dogs, Humans, Cattle, Hens, Insects, Birds, reproduction takes place through internal fertilisation.

WHAT ARE THE THREATS TO LIFE ON EARTH ?

We have studied that both biotic and abiotic components interact with each other and form a delicate balance for life to sustain on Earth. Any interference in this disrupts the equilibrium which has a cascading effect.

Today, due to the human activities, Earth is facing the triple planetary crisis of :
- Climate change
- Biodiversity loss
- Pollution

Changes in global temperature, oxygen level and ozone layer is putting life at risk.

Climate Change

Long-term shifts in temperature and weather patterns is known as climate change. Fossil fuels like coal, oil and gas, are the largest contributor to global climate change. Burning fossil fuels emits greenhouse gas like carbon dioxide and methane. As these emissions blanket the Earth, they trap the Sun's heat. This leads to global warming and climate change. Warmer temperatures over time are melting ice caps and raising sea levels causing flood in many coastal cities. This is changing weather patterns, leading to disappearance of many plants and animals, thus disrupting the usual balance of nature. This is posing many more risks to human beings and all other forms of life on Earth.

Biodiversity Loss

Biodiversity loss refers to the decline or disappearance of biological diversity; the variety of living beings that inhabit the planet.

Biodiversity has declined at an alarming rate in recent years, largely as a result of human activity. Climate change, pollution, destruction of habitat due to soil pollution and deforestation are some of the causes of biodiversity loss. As we have seen in the previous chapter, even if one species in the food chain vanishes, it upsets the entire ecosystem.

Pollution

Pollution is one of the major factors that has created an imbalance in nature. Contamination of air by smoke, dust and harmful gases like carbon dioxide has led to global warming. This has created health problems for human beings (breathing problem, asthma, bronchitis) changed the pattern of crop cultivation, adversely affected the monsoon rains, led to smog and acid rain, melted the polar ice.

Consumption of polluted water causes diseases like cholera, dysentery, hepatitis and many other life—threatening diseases. Mixing of toxic substances like mercury, lead etc. in water bodies is harming both human beings and aquatic life.

Dumping of garbage and poisonous chemicals in landfills; domestic, industrial, agricultural and plastic waste in soil are causing soil pollution. It erodes the soil and makes the land unfit for cultivation. Organisms like Earthworms that live in soil, lose their habitat.

STRATEGIES FOR A SUSTAINABLE FUTURE

Hydrosphere, biosphere, atmosphere and geosphere are all interconnected. Damage to one naturally affects the other.

Improving the quality of fuels in automobiles, replacing wood, coal and kerosene with LPG for domestic use; using better technology to control pollutants in the emissions and planting more trees; proper treatment of wastage before their release; use of organic fertilizers and manure; using renewable energy like solar and wind; choosing environmentally friendly means of travel are some ways in which we can save the Earth and preserve biodiversity.

As the world is becoming more aware about this global threat, countries are making agreements to protect the environment. Following are some Global agreements :

  • The Montreal Protocol (1987) to reduce harmful chemicals like chlorofluorocarbons in order to recover the ozone layer.
  • The Earth summit (1992) that led to international efforts on climate change and biodiversity.
  • The Kyoto Protocol (2005) and Paris Agreement (2015) aimed at reducing greenhouse gas emissions by countries.

The goals set in the Paris Agreement to limit global warming to below 1.5°C has not yet been met.

It is not just up to the world leaders, we, at the local level can also make a difference. Each one of us can contribute by reusing and recycling items like clothes as well as reducing waste and pollution. Every effort counts, even the smallest and simplest act like saving energy and water.

It is high time we act responsibly and work towards protecting our unique planet Earth.

KEY WORDS

  • Greenhouse effect : Process through which heat is trapped near Earth's surface by greenhouse gases.
  • Habitable zone (Goldilocks zone) : The range of distances from the Sun (or another star) over which water remains liquid.
  • Ozone layer : Layer in the atmosphere that acts as a shield, blocking harmful ultraviolet (UV) rays from the Sun that can damage life on Earth.
  • Cosmic rays : Tiny, high energy particles coming from space and hitting the Earth.
  • Solar wind : Tiny, high energy charged particles coming to the Earth from the Sun.
  • Hydrosphere : All the water that covers the Earth's surface in the form of different water bodies.

  • Geosphere : The solid parts of the Earth, including materials like rocks, soils and minerals.

  • Geodiversity : The natural variety of geological (i.e., rocks, minerals, and fossils) features, soil, hydrological features (water bodies) along with landforms and processes that shape them.

  • Biosphere : The sum of all ecosystems on Earth, representing the thin zone where life exists.
  • Genetic material (genes) : Instructions by parents to their offspring about the process to develop from a single cell, which are stored inside every cell of a living being.
  • Vegetative propagation : A kind of asexual reproduction where plants reproduce when any of their parts (leaf, stem or root) is planted in the soil.
  • Gametes : Specialised reproductive cells made by each parent.
  • Pollination : The process of pollens being carried to another flower by wind, insects or animals.
  • Fertilisation : Combination of male and female gamete to form a zygote that becomes a seed.

E. Assertion-Reason

For question numbers 1 to 5, two statements are given—one labelled Assertion (A) and the other labelled Reason (R). Select the correct answer to these questions from the codes (a), (b), (c) and (d) as given below:

(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of the Assertion.
(b) Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation of the Assertion.
(c) Assertion is true but Reason is false.
(d) Assertion is false but Reason is true.

One has been done for you as an example.

EXAMPLE

Assertion (A): Earth is the only planet known to support life.
Reason (R): Earth has water, suitable atmosphere, and ideal temperature range.
Explanation: (a) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of the Assertion.

  1. Assertion (A): Earth’s atmosphere is rich in oxygen.
    Reason (R): Carbon dioxide makes up more than 20% of Earth’s atmosphere.

  2. Assertion (A): Earth lies in the habitable zone of the solar system.
    Reason (R): The temperature on Earth allows liquid water to exist.

  3. Assertion (A): Earth is the fourth planet from the Sun.
    Reason (R): Venus is the second planet from the Sun.

  4. Assertion (A): Hydrosphere includes all forms of water on Earth.
    Reason (R): Earth’s size helps it retain an atmosphere.

  5. Assertion (A): Asexual reproduction involves one parent.
    Reason (R): It produces genetically different offspring.

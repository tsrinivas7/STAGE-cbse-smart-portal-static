Nature of Matter : Elements, Compounds, and Mixtures

Everything around us is made up of matter, which consists of tiny particles. Most things are made up of two or more substances mixed together.

WHAT ARE MIXTURES ?

mixtures — Materials which contain two or more substances in any proportion.
components or constituents — The various substances present in a mixture; each retains its properties and does not react chemically with others.

  • A mixture may be solid, liquid, or gas.
  • Components do not react chemically with each other.
  • Each constituent retains its individual properties (e.g., sugar and sand in a mixture).
  • Examples include sprout salad, soil, and pond water.

Classification of Mixtures

All mixtures are divided into two broad categories based on the distribution of their particles.

Non-uniform Mixtures

non-uniform mixtures or heterogeneous mixtures — Mixtures in which particles of the substances present can be seen easily with the naked eye or magnifying device.

  • Particles are visible separately.
  • Examples include water + oil, chalk in water, dust in air, and sand + iron filings.
  • Properties of components remain distinct.

Uniform Mixtures

uniform mixtures or homogeneous mixtures — Mixtures in which the particles of the substances present cannot be seen separately even with the help of a microscope.

  • Particles are uniformly distributed.
  • Examples include solutions of sugar/salt in water, cold drinks, and pure air.
  • Properties appear consistent throughout the mixture.

Alloys

alloy — A uniform mixture of metals or a metal with another element (e.g., stainless steel, brass, bronze).

  • Stainless steel is a mixture of iron, nickel, chromium, and carbon.
  • Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc.
  • Bronze is an alloy of copper and tin.
  • Ancient Indian texts mention the use of alloys ("mishraloha") for medicinal purposes.

Is Air a Mixture ?

  • Air is a uniform mixture of many gases.
  • Main components: Nitrogen (78%) and Oxygen (21%).
  • Contains small quantities of carbon dioxide, water vapour, noble gases, dust, and smoke.
  • Air shows properties of all the components present in it.

Activity 1: To Show the Presence of Carbon Dioxide in Air

  • Lime water — A solution of calcium hydroxide formed by reacting calcium oxide with water.
  • Calcium hydroxide reacts with carbon dioxide to form calcium carbonate (insoluble white particles), turning lime water milky.
  • This proves the presence of carbon dioxide in the air.

Activity 2: To Show the Presence of Dust Particles in the Air

  • Tiny shining particles moving in a beam of sunlight are dust particles suspended in air.
  • Placing a black sheet near an open window allows dust to settle, proving their presence.
  • Dust particles are considered pollutants along with gases like carbon monoxide and sulfur oxide.

Adulteration

adulteration — An illegal practice in which cheaper or inferior materials are added to a product to increase quantity or reduce costs.
* Lowers quality and can be harmful to health.
* In science, separating mixtures aims to obtain pure substances, avoiding adulteration.

WHAT ARE PURE SUBSTANCES ?

pure substance — A single substance of a definite composition containing particles of only one type.
impure substance — A substance in which some other substances are present in smaller amounts.

  • Has a fixed melting point or boiling point.
  • Has characteristic taste, colour, melting point, and boiling point.
  • Examples: Pure iron, pure copper, pure gold, pure oxygen.

WHAT ARE THE TYPES OF PURE SUBSTANCES ?

Pure substances are classified into Elements and Compounds.

Activity: Constituents of Water

  • electrolysis of water — The process of passing electricity through water to break it down into hydrogen and oxygen.
  • Water decomposes into Hydrogen and Oxygen gases.
  • Hydrogen burns with a pop sound; Oxygen makes a flame glow brighter.
  • Inference: Water is composed of two different constituents—hydrogen and oxygen.

ELEMENTS

element — The simplest form of matter, which cannot be split into simpler substances by any physical or chemical method.
atoms — Same kind of particles that compose an element.
molecule — A stable particle formed when two or more atoms of the same kind combine.

  • Elements are basic constituents of all matter.
  • Atoms of most elements cannot exist independently (form molecules).
  • Examples: Hydrogen, Oxygen, Gold, Silver, Copper, Nitrogen, Sulfur, Carbon.
  • metals — Elements like gold, silver, copper, iron, aluminium, magnesium.
  • non-metals — Elements like hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, phosphorus, carbon, sulfur.
  • metalloids — Elements like boron, silicon, germanium with intermediate properties.
  • Mercury and Bromine are liquid at room temperature.
  • Eleven elements (all non-metals) exist in gaseous state at room temperature.

COMPOUNDS

compound — A pure substance formed by the chemical combination of two or more elements in a definite proportion.

  • Obtained by chemical combination in a definite proportion.
  • Properties are the same throughout.
  • Chemical and physical properties are entirely different from constituent elements (e.g., water extinguishes fire, while hydrogen burns and oxygen supports combustion).
  • Cannot be separated by physical methods (e.g., water needs electrolysis).
  • Examples: Water, Sodium Chloride (Salt), Sugar.

Activity 4: To Separate the Elements of Sugar

  • Heating sugar causes it to char and turn blackish (carbon).
  • Small droplets of water appear inside the tube.
  • Inference: Sugar decomposes into carbon and water (which contains hydrogen and oxygen).
  • Sugar is a chemical compound consisting of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen.

Activity 5: To Know More About Compounds

  • Sample A (Mixture): Iron filings + Sulfur powder. Retains properties (attracted by magnet, reacts with acid to release hydrogen).
  • Sample B (Compound): Iron sulfide (formed by heating Sample A). New substance with different properties (not attracted by magnet, releases rotten egg smell gas with acid).
  • Demonstrates that compounds have unique properties different from their constituent elements.

HOW DO WE USE ELEMENTS, COMPOUNDS AND MIXTURES ?

  • Elements: Used in construction (iron, copper, aluminium) and vehicles.
  • Compounds: Essential for survival (water), life processes (carbon dioxide).
  • Mixtures: Support life (air), used in building materials (wood, concrete, alloys).
  • Graphene aerogel: Lightest material, used as environmental cleaner and energy-saving devices.

WHAT ARE MINERALS ?

minerals — Any natural element or compound found in the Earth's crust having a fixed chemical composition.
ores — Minerals from which metals are extracted.
native minerals — Pure elements found naturally (e.g., gold).
metallic minerals — Minerals containing metals (e.g., lead, iron, copper).
non-metallic minerals — Minerals containing non-metals (e.g., sulfur, carbon).

  • Most rocks are mixtures of minerals.
  • Some minerals are gemstones (Diamond, Sapphire, Emerald).
  • Industrial minerals include Limestone, Dolomite, Gypsum.
  • Energy minerals include Coal, Lignite.
  • Cement is made from calcite, quartz, alumina, and iron oxide.

II. LOGIC-BASED QUESTIONS (Page 172)

1. Sugar crystals obtained from sugarcane and beetroot are mixed together. Will it be a pure substance or a mixture? Give reason.
Answer: It will be a mixture. Even though both are sugar, mixing them involves physical combination without chemical bonding, and they retain their individual properties.

2. Hydrogen and oxygen are considered elements, whereas water is not considered as an element. Give reason.
Answer: Hydrogen and oxygen consist of only one type of atom and cannot be broken down further. Water consists of two different types of atoms (hydrogen and oxygen) chemically combined in a fixed ratio, making it a compound.

* Uniform: Soda water, Filtered tea, Air.
* Non-uniform: Soil, Wood.

4. A scientist mixes iron filings and sulfur powder and heats the mixture. A new substance is formed. What type of change has occurred and what is the resulting substance?
Answer: A chemical change has occurred. The resulting substance is iron sulfide (ferrous sulfide).

5. You are given three samples: Sample A: Pure gold, Sample B: Salt (NaCl), Sample C: Soil. Classify each as an element, compound, or mixture. Give reason for your classification.
Answer:
* Sample A (Pure gold): Element. Reason: Made of only one type of atom (gold).
* Sample B (Salt): Compound. Reason: Made of sodium and chlorine atoms chemically combined in a fixed ratio.
* Sample C (Soil): Mixture. Reason: Contains sand, clay, minerals, organic matter mixed physically without fixed ratio.

6. Why is water considered a compound, but air is considered a mixture? Give three logical reasons.
Answer:
1. Composition: Water has a fixed ratio of hydrogen to oxygen (2:1), while air has variable proportions of gases.
2. Bonding: Water elements are chemically bonded; air components are physically mixed.
3. Properties: Water has properties different from hydrogen and oxygen; air retains the properties of its component gases.

7. A solution of salt in water appears to be a single substance, but it is actually a mixture. Explain this logically.
Answer: Although it looks uniform, salt and water retain their individual properties (salt can be recovered by evaporation). They are not chemically bonded in a fixed ratio, fitting the definition of a mixture.

Two or more elements combine together chemically in a definite proportion to form a pure substance called compound. For example, water is a chemical compound made up of hydrogen and oxygen combined in the ratio of 2 : 1.

A compound has the following characteristics :
* It is obtained by the chemical combination of two or more elements in a definite proportion.
* The properties of compounds are the same throughout.
* The chemical and physical properties of a compound are entirely different from those of the constituent elements.

For example, hydrogen is a combustible gas, while oxygen is a supporter of combustion, but their compound water is neither combustible nor a supporter of combustion. On the contrary, water extinguishes fire.

Similarly, sodium is a soft metal while chlorine is a hazardous gas. They both combine in the ratio of 1 : 1 to form a harmless taste enhancing substance called sodium chloride. Dissolved sodium chloride (common salt) can be separated from water by the physical process of evaporation.

Both common salt and sugar are compounds.

WHAT ARE MINERALS ?

Minerals are any natural element or compound found in the Earth's crust, having a fixed chemical composition.

Most rocks are a mixture of minerals which can be viewed with the eyes or by using a magnifying glass or a microscope. Some minerals are pure elements which are called native minerals.

The minerals containing metals are called metallic minerals. Minerals from which metals are extracted are called ores. Metals like lead, iron, copper, aluminium, gold are extracted from their ores.

Minerals that have non-metals like sulfur, carbon etc. in them are called non-metallic minerals.

Some of the non-metallic minerals are in the form of crystals. They are hard and can be polished to make gemstones. Diamond, sapphire, emerald are some gemstones which are used to make jewellery. Limestone, dolomite, gypsum are industrial minerals. Coal, lignite are energy minerals used for power generation.

Most minerals are compounds made up of more than one elements, like quartz, calcite, mica, pyroxene and olivine. Cement is made from calcite, quartz, alumina and iron oxide, which are either minerals or are obtained from minerals. Talcum powder is made from mineral talc.

Core Concept

Conceptual Learning

Nature of Matter : Elements, Compounds, and Mixtures

Mixtures

Materials containing two or more substances in any proportion, e.g., mixture of sand + iron filings

Types

  • Based on distribution of components
    • Uniform/Homogeneous mixture : Components cannot be seen through naked eye, e.g., sugar + water, salt + water
    • Non-uniform/Heterogeneous mixture : Components can be seen separately through naked eye, e.g., sand + clay, salt + sugar
  • Based on physical states
    • Gas and gas e.g., Natural gas
    • Liquid and gas e.g., Fog
    • Solid and gas e.g., Smoke, dust particles in air
    • Liquid and liquid e.g., Honey and water
    • Solid and liquid e.g., Sugar in water, seawater
    • Solid and solid e.g., Vegetable salad, alloys

Components of a mixture may be

  • Impure substance : A substance with indefinite composition
  • Pure substance : A substance with definite composition
    • Types
      • Elements : Substances made up of a single substance with definite composition, having particles of one type only, e.g., pure gold, oxygen, iron, nitrogen, etc.
      • Compounds : Formed by chemical combination of two or more elements, having properties different from those of constituent elements, e.g., water, sodium chloride (common salt), etc.

Uses of Elements, Compounds and Mixtures

  • Elements like iron, aluminium, copper are used in buildings, bridges, vehicles, etc.
  • Compounds like water, carbon dioxide, etc. are useful in many life processes in plants and animals.
  • Mixtures like air support life, alloys are used to make many useful products.

Minerals

Naturally-occurring elements or compounds found in Earth's crust with fixed composition
* Native minerals : Pure elements e.g., gold
* Ores : Minerals from which metals are extracted e.g., aluminium
* Gemstones : Hard non-metallic minerals that can be polished to make jewellery

KEY WORDS

  • Mixture: Materials which contain two or more substances in any proportion.
  • Uniform mixture: Mixture in which particles of substances present are uniformly distributed and cannot be seen.

  • Compound: New substances formed by combining different elements in a fixed ratio which have entirely different properties from their constituent elements.

The word equation for the reaction is : Iron + Sulfur —→ Iron sulfide.

In original mixture, iron filings and sulfur powder are physically mixed. They retain their individual properties, and the mixture can be separated by physical means like using a magnet.

Ferrous sulfide (FeS) : Heating the mixture causes a chemical reaction. Iron and sulfur combine to form a new substance, iron sulfide which is a compound.

Difference in properties : Ferrous sulfide has its unique properties that are distinct from those of iron and sulfur. For example, it is a black solid, whereas iron is typically gray, and sulfur is yellow. It is also not attracted to magnets like iron.
